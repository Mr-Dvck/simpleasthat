# **The Genesis of Synthetic Speech: A Comprehensive Analysis of ElevenLabs’ Founders, Cultural Catalyst, and Early Corporate Evolution**

The emergence of ElevenLabs within the global artificial intelligence landscape represents more than a mere technological advancement in speech synthesis; it signifies a fundamental shift in the relationship between human identity, linguistic authenticity, and digital accessibility. Founded in 2022, the company has rapidly ascended to a position of indisputable authority in the AI voice generation market, which is projected to expand from $4.1 billion in 2025 to over $20.7 billion by 2031\.1 This trajectory was solidified in February 2026 when the company closed a $500 million Series D funding round led by Sequoia, reaching a valuation of $11 billion—a more than threefold increase from its previous year’s valuation of $3.3 billion.1 However, the roots of this multi-billion dollar enterprise lie not in the affluent suburbs of Silicon Valley, but in the shared frustrations of two childhood friends navigating the unique media landscape of post-communist Poland.2

## **The Founders: A Synergy of Technical Rigor and Strategic Vision**

The architecture of ElevenLabs is built upon the lifelong partnership of Piotr Dąbkowski and Mateusz "Mati" Staniszewski. Their relationship, spanning more than twelve years before the company’s inception, began at Copernicus High School in Warsaw, Poland.5 This early connection fostered a shared curiosity for technology and innovation that would eventually bridge the gap between academic research and commercial deployment.5

### **Mateusz Staniszewski: The Strategic Architect**

Mateusz Staniszewski, who serves as the Chief Executive Officer of ElevenLabs, brought a diverse professional background characterized by the strategic implementation of complex data systems. Staniszewski’s academic foundation was established at Imperial College London, where he earned a First-Class Honours degree in Mathematics.8 This quantitative rigor became the bedrock for his early career in business intelligence and product development.8

Staniszewski’s professional path included roles at Opera Software in Oslo and London, and subsequently at BlackRock, where he contributed to the development and launch of the Aladdin Wealth platform.8 However, his four-year tenure as a Deployment Strategist at Palantir Technologies was arguably the most critical precursor to ElevenLabs.8 At Palantir, Staniszewski focused on large-scale data integration and analytics projects for Fortune 500 companies and government entities, gaining invaluable insights into the ethical, regulatory, and operational challenges of deploying frontier technologies at scale.8 This experience would later inform ElevenLabs’ ability to transition rapidly from a consumer-facing tool to a critical piece of enterprise infrastructure.7

### **Piotr Dąbkowski: The Machine Learning Pioneer**

Piotr Dąbkowski, the Chief Technology Officer, provided the deep technical expertise necessary to overcome the long-standing hurdles of robotic-sounding speech synthesis. Dąbkowski pursued his higher education at the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge, specializing in engineering and machine learning.5 His academic prowess was demonstrated early when his graduate thesis on AI-based image detection was published at the Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), a top-tier machine learning conference, years before the mainstream explosion of large language models.3

Before co-founding ElevenLabs, Dąbkowski served as a machine learning engineer at Google, where he worked on scalable models within Google Shopping and other infrastructure projects.7 His work at Google, alongside stints at technical infrastructure firms like Tessian, equipped him with the ability to build sophisticated models from first principles.3 His technical philosophy centered on the idea that voice models should learn the nuances of speech through pattern recognition rather than being limited by hard-coded features like pitch or gender sliders.10

## **Cultural Catalyst: The Polish "Lektor" and the Frustration of Inauthenticity**

The genesis of ElevenLabs is inextricably linked to the "lektor" phenomenon, a distinct feature of Polish broadcasting that has shaped the media consumption habits of the country since the communist era.14 For Staniszewski and Dąbkowski, growing up in Poland meant watching Hollywood blockbusters and American sitcoms through the filter of a single, monotone narrator.2

### **The Mechanics of the Lektor Tradition**

In Poland, foreign films are rarely dubbed with a full cast of actors. Instead, a voice-over artist, known as the lektor, reads the Polish translation over the original audio track.15 The original soundtrack remains audible at a lower volume, creating a layered audio experience where the viewer hears the original actor’s voice and the lektor’s monotone narration simultaneously.15 Historically, this method was a cost-effective alternative to full dubbing during the Cold War era, when resources for multi-actor translation were limited.14

The lektor’s delivery is famously neutral. They read every character’s lines—man, woman, and child—without attempting to mimic emotional tones, accents, or vocal characteristics.15 Prominent lektors like Maciej Gudowski or the late Tomasz Knapik became iconic figures in Polish culture, their husky, emotionless voices becoming synonymous with foreign entertainment.17 However, for tech-savvy teenagers like Staniszewski and Dąbkowski, this method was deeply unsatisfying.2 They felt that the lektor stripped performances of their original nuance, turning the high-stakes drama of actors like Leonardo DiCaprio or Scarlett Johansson into a flat, robotic experience.2

### **From Childhood Annoyance to Professional Mission**

The founders’ dislike for poor localization was more than just a preference; it was a realization that technology had failed to preserve human emotion across linguistic barriers.4 They observed that while visual effects had advanced to create entire digital worlds, the vocal interfaces of the early 21st century—such as Siri and Alexa—remained stubbornly robotic and obviously artificial.3 This shared frustration became a professional mission: to build a generative AI platform that could replicate the subtle inflections, breathing patterns, and emotional cadence of the human voice in any language.2

## **The Beginning History of the Company: From Weekend Projects to a Global Entity**

ElevenLabs did not emerge as a fully-formed business plan but as the culmination of long-term collaboration. Even while working their high-stakes jobs at Google and Palantir, Staniszewski and Dąbkowski maintained a "weekend project" culture, where they explored various technical problems that interested them.13

### **The "Eureka" Moment: The Public Speaking Coach Pivot**

One of their most significant weekend endeavors was the development of an AI-powered public speaking coach.19 This project was designed to analyze a user's speech and provide feedback on their tonality, pace, and delivery.19 During the development of this tool, the pair realized that the underlying technology—the ability of a machine to understand and reconstruct the nuances of human speech—had far broader implications than just coaching.19

They recognized that the same models they were building to analyze voice could be reversed to generate it. This led them back to their childhood frustration with Polish dubbing. They realized that if they could create an AI that captured authentic character and emotion, they could "finally save Polish people from terrible dubbing".13 In May 2022, they made the definitive decision to leave their stable careers, pooling their personal savings to work full-time on the problem of synthetic speech.19

### **The Stealth Phase and Incorporation**

ElevenLabs was officially co-founded in April 2022 as a research-first company.3 The founders initially worked in a "stealth mode" in London, refining their models and building the core infrastructure before any public reveal.4 During this period, their primary focus was not on immediate monetization but on solving the fundamental technical challenge: why did existing text-to-speech technologies sound so fake?.5

Their approach was to conduct in-house research to understand the "humanness" of speech.5 Instead of fine-tuning existing datasets, they developed proprietary models from scratch that could capture elements like natural pauses, laughter, and conversational fillers.5 This research-centric philosophy was a gamble, as it delayed their entry into the market during a period when AI was exploding in popularity, but it ensured that when they did launch, their product would be significantly superior to anything else available.8

### **The Symbolism of the Name: ElevenLabs**

The name "ElevenLabs" was chosen carefully to reflect the founders' ambitions and personality. According to Staniszewski, the name celebrates several shared passions:

* **The Number 11:** Staniszewski noted a mathematical affinity for the number 11, specifically its "great divisibility" properties and aesthetic symmetry.25  
* **Apollo 11:** A reference to the moon landing, symbolizing a "giant leap" for humanity in the field of audio technology.25  
* **Spinal Tap:** A nod to the mockumentary *This Is Spinal Tap*, specifically the scene where an amplifier is modified to go "up to eleven," representing their goal of pushing speech technology beyond its traditional limits.25  
* **Vocal Turing Test:** Internally, the name represented the lab where they would attempt to "turn the dial" on synthetic speech until it was indistinguishable from the human original.20

## **Early Funding and the Path to Unicorn Status**

The company’s early financial journey was characterized by rapid investor interest driven by the obvious quality of their initial prototypes.12 In the early months, the founders considered various funding routes, including startup accelerators, but eventually opted for traditional venture capital that aligned with their research-first mission.12

### **Pre-Seed and Series A: Validation of the Vision**

In January 2023, ElevenLabs announced its first external funding—a $2 million pre-seed round.8 This round was led by Credo Ventures and joined by Concept Ventures, along with several high-profile angel investors.12 The founders used a 14-slide pitch deck to secure this funding, emphasizing the massive gap in the market for realistic audio.12 Just five months after launching their beta platform in January 2023, the company had already attracted over one million users.5

The rapid user adoption quickly led to a Series A round in June 2023\.12 Led by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) and supported by other "heavy hitters" in the venture space, ElevenLabs raised $19 million at a valuation of approximately $100 million.4 This capital was immediately deployed to scale their research team and expand their language support beyond English.6

### **Series B to Series D: Scaling a Global Infrastructure**

The subsequent funding rounds followed in quick succession as ElevenLabs transitioned from a creator tool to a global infrastructure provider. By January 2024, the company reached unicorn status with an $80 million Series B round, valuing the venture at $1.1 billion.3 This round saw participation from Sequoia Capital for the first time, signaling a shift toward long-term enterprise dominance.7

The momentum continued through 2025 and 2026\. In early 2025, the company raised $180 million in Series C funding, reaching a $3.3 billion valuation.1 Finally, the Series D round in February 2026, which brought in $500 million, gave ElevenLabs the $11 billion valuation that solidified its control over the AI voice submarket.1

## **Product Evolution: Beyond Simple Text-to-Speech**

As the company grew, the founders’ vision expanded from a dubbing tool to a full-stack audio operating system.29 This evolution was driven by the realization that realistic voice was not just a product in itself, but a new interface for interacting with technology.2

### **Core Technological Pillars**

The ElevenLabs platform is organized into three primary product lines: ElevenCreative, ElevenAgents, and ElevenAPI.20 This structure allows them to serve individual creators, enterprise customer service departments, and developers simultaneously.20

* **ElevenCreative:** This platform is designed for media professionals and creators. It includes the flagship Text-to-Speech engine, which supports over 70 languages, and the AI Dubbing tool, which can translate video content while preserving the original speaker's voice and emotional performance.1  
* **ElevenAgents:** Launched as the company shifted toward conversational AI, this product allows businesses to deploy natural-sounding AI assistants.20 Companies like Square and Revolut use these agents to handle complex customer queries with latency times under one second, significantly outperforming traditional automated systems.1  
* **ElevenAPI:** This suite provides the "rails" for the future of audio content. It allows developers to integrate hyper-realistic voice generation into their own apps, ranging from personalized tutoring to real-time translation in video games.2

### **The Iconic Voice Marketplace: A Strategic Bridge to Hollywood**

One of the most significant developments in ElevenLabs' early history was the launch of the Iconic Voice Marketplace.1 In an industry often characterized by tension between AI developers and the creative community, ElevenLabs took a proactive approach by building a platform for the authorized use of celebrity voices.1

By partnering with actors like Matthew McConaughey and Michael Caine, as well as the estates of figures like Maya Angelou and Alan Turing, ElevenLabs created a two-sided marketplace where artists can monetize their digital likeness while retaining control over how it is used.1 This strategy was not just about technology; it was a masterclass in strategic alignment, ensuring that the company worked with the creative industry rather than against it.11 McConaughey, in particular, became a vocal proponent of the platform, even taking an equity stake in the company.1

## **Navigating the Ethical and Regulatory Landscape**

The rapid rise of ElevenLabs has not been without significant challenges. The same technology that allows for realistic film dubbing also possesses the potential for widespread misuse.4

### **Misuse and Deepfake Controversies**

In early 2023, shortly after the beta launch, the company faced its first major crisis when users on the message board 4chan used ElevenLabs to generate hateful messages mimicking celebrities like Emma Watson.12 Further reports documented how the technology could be used to malicious ends, such as generating threats or racist remarks in seconds.31 Perhaps most alarmingly, investigative reporters proved that a cloned voice could be convincing enough to fool bank authentication systems.31

The founders responded by prioritizing safety and traceability. They implemented a system where users must be verified to create custom voices and released an AI speech classifier tool that allows the public to determine if an audio clip was generated by ElevenLabs.6 This proactive stance on safety was crucial in maintaining the trust of both regulators and enterprise clients, such as financial services firms that require PCI Level 1 certification.4

### **Intellectual Property and Actor Concerns**

Beyond deepfakes, ElevenLabs had to navigate the concerns of the voice acting community. Many actors expressed fear that their voices could be sampled without consent and used to promote content they did not endorse.31 The founders addressed this by establishing strict moderation policies and focusing on licensed data for their foundational models.8 For instance, their music model, Eleven Music, was built after 18 months of negotiations with major labels to ensure an agreement that worked for both the technology and the artists.11

## **Organizational Culture: A Global Hub Outside Silicon Valley**

ElevenLabs’ success is also a testament to its unique organizational structure. Despite its massive valuation, the company maintains a lean and geographically distributed team.6

### **Remote-First Philosophy**

Mati Staniszewski and Piotr Dąbkowski consciously chose to build a global, remote-first company rather than constraining themselves to a single tech hub.6 By early 2026, the company’s team of over 20 people was working from locations across Europe (including Slovakia, Poland, Germany, and Switzerland), as well as the US and Asia.6 This approach allowed them to tap into high-quality technical talent in regions like Poland, where familiarity with startup practices might be lower but technical aptitude and passion for the subject are exceptionally high.6

### **Hybrid Hubs and Strategic Presence**

While remote-first, the company established strategic hubs in London and New York.1 The New York headquarters on Madison Avenue and a significant 12,000 square foot office in SoHo provide a base for their US operations, which was essential for incorporating in the US and accessing its investment ecosystem.6 This "transatlantic" approach required a vision of building a global company from the outset, rather than simply trying to succeed in one specific market.23

## **Impact and Future Outlook: Toward an AI Audio Operating System**

By early 2026, the impact of ElevenLabs had permeated multiple industries, from education to high-end cinematic production. The company ended 2025 with more than $330 million in annual recurring revenue, exceeding its initial goals.1

### **Enterprise Adoption and Real-World Use Cases**

The versatility of ElevenLabs' technology is evident in its diverse range of partners and use cases:

* **Media and Publishing:** *Time Magazine*, *The Washington Post*, and HarperCollins use the platform to generate localized audio content and audiobooks.1  
* **Education:** Duolingo utilizes ElevenLabs' voice infrastructure to provide more expressive and natural-sounding character voices for language learners.1  
* **Government Services:** The Ukrainian government has partnered with ElevenLabs to add audio interfaces to public services and to provide flawlessly translated English, Ukrainian, and Russian audio for official interviews, preserving the speaker's original voice.1  
* **Gaming and Tech:** Companies like Nvidia and Epic Games use the platform's API to enable developers to build immersive audio experiences.1

### **Future Expansion and IPO Plans**

With $781 million in total funding as of early 2026, ElevenLabs is looking toward an eventual Initial Public Offering (IPO).1 Staniszewski has stated that the company hopes to be IPO-ready within the next five years, emphasizing that they remain "hungry" despite their rapid success.1 Plans for physical expansion include opening new offices in Europe, Asia, Australia, and South America to maintain their lead over competitors.1

The founders’ long-term vision is for ElevenLabs to become the "rails" for all digital communication.7 As voice increasingly becomes the primary user interface for generative AI, ElevenLabs' addressable market is expected to grow exponentially.2 From theme park operators cloning animated characters for animatronic robots to contact centers managing millions of simultaneous customer interactions, the potential applications for hyper-realistic, low-latency synthetic speech are virtually limitless.2

## **Conclusion: A Movement Driven by Human Connection**

The story of ElevenLabs is a narrative of how personal frustration can be transformed into industrial innovation through the application of deep technical rigor and strategic deployment.4 Piotr Dąbkowski and Mateusz Staniszewski did not simply build a product; they initiated a movement that is reshaping how we think about voice, language, and identity in the digital age.4 By starting from the "uniquely Polish horror" of bad dubbing, they successfully identified a universal human need: the desire for authentic, emotionally resonant communication that transcends linguistic borders.4 As ElevenLabs continues to push toward its goal of passing the vocal Turing test across every major language, it stands as a testament to the power of childhood friendship, cultural perspective, and the relentless pursuit of making machines sound truly human.4
